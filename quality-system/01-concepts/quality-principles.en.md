# Quality Values & Principles

## Quality Values

Our core values we firmly believe in:

1. **Built-in Quality** — Quality is born in building, not in inspection.
2. **Continuous Improvement** — Meeting the bar is the start; going beyond is the pursuit.
3. **Process Serves Product** — Process serves the product rather than constraining it.
4. **Domain-Driven** — Deeply understand the business domain, and let domain knowledge drive design and decisions.

## Nine Quality Principles

### Principle 1: Quality Is Everyone’s Responsibility

It is not the responsibility of a single role. From product managers to developers to test engineers, everyone is accountable for quality.

**Cultural Foundation** — Quality is not the QA department’s job; it is a matter of team culture.

### Principle 2: Domain Understanding Is the Foundation of Quality

The depth of business understanding determines the upper bound of quality. Without understanding the complexity of the education domain, we cannot deliver truly valuable software.

**Cognitive Starting Point** — Understand the domain first, then build the product.

### Principle 3: Requirements Are the Starting Point of Quality

Correct understanding comes before correct implementation. Misunderstood requirements spread into design deviations, coding rework, and ineffective testing.

**Value Definition** — Requirements define “doing the right thing”; all other phases ensure “doing the thing right”.

### Principle 4: Simple Design Beats Over-Engineering

Complexity is the enemy of quality. Every unnecessary layer of abstraction and every unused extension point becomes future maintenance burden and a breeding ground for defects.

**Design Guideline** — Solve today’s problems with the simplest solution; do not add complexity for a hypothetical future.

### Principle 5: Shift Validation Left

Continuous integration is the baseline. The earlier a defect is found, the lower the cost to fix it. Finding requirement errors in the requirements phase, design defects in the design phase, and code issues in the coding phase increases cost step by step.

**Build Assurance** — Push validation to the earliest possible moment rather than delaying it to the latest.

### Principle 6: Security Is a Default Property

Security is not an add-on feature. It must be considered in requirements, modeled in design, and enforced in coding, rather than patched right before release.

**Security Baseline** — Security is the foundation of the product, not the decoration.

### Principle 7: Changes and Releases Are Controlled

They must be traceable and repeatable. Every change has a rationale and review record; every release is repeatable and has rollback capability.

**Delivery Assurance** — Controlled changes and releases are the foundation of stable delivery.

### Principle 8: Metrics Drive Improvement

Quality goals must be measurable, and problems must trigger improvement. What cannot be measured cannot be improved; every production incident is a signal to improve the system.

**Improvement Mechanism** — Speak with data; drive with facts.

### Principle 9: The System Evolves Itself

Review and prune regularly; resist rigidity. The system serves the product; when the product or environment changes, the system must evolve accordingly.

**System Vitality** — The best system is a living set of rules, not dead clauses.
